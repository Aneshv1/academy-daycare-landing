### Post 17 — Place Command Tip
**Type:** What's New | **CTA:** Learn More

One of the most underrated commands: "place."

Teach your dog to go to a designated spot (bed, mat, cot) and stay there calmly. It's a game-changer for guests arriving, dinner time, or just relaxing at home.

Start with 10 seconds on the bed. Reward. Build up gradually. Consistency is everything.

Our Day & Train program teaches place command as a core skill — most dogs master it within 2 weeks.

### Post 18 — Leash Walking Tip
**Type:** What's New | **CTA:** Learn More

Tired of your dog pulling on the leash?

Here's the simplest fix: stop walking the moment the leash goes tight. Wait for slack, then continue. No yanking. No correcting. Just stop.

It's painful for the first few walks. Most dogs get it within a week.

If you need faster results, our Day & Train program includes structured leash work every day. Loose leash walking is one of the first things we teach.

### Post 20 — Separation Anxiety Tip
**Type:** What's New | **CTA:** Learn More

Does your dog panic when you leave the house?

Separation anxiety is real — it's not your dog being dramatic. It's a genuine fear response.

Quick tip: practice "boring departures." Pick up your keys and sit back down. Put on shoes and watch TV. Desensitize your dog to the cues that mean you're leaving.

Consistent daycare also helps. When your dog has a structured routine outside the house, being away from you becomes normal — not scary.

### Post 21 — Summer Heat Tip
**Type:** What's New | **CTA:** Learn More

Hot day? Skip the midday walk.

In summer, pavement can hit 60°C+ when the air is only 30°C. That's enough to burn your dog's paws in seconds.

Walk before 8 AM or after 7 PM. Use grass when possible. Bring water.

Our facility is climate-controlled, so your dog stays active and cool all day — even during heat waves. Outdoor time is supervised and adjusted based on weather.
