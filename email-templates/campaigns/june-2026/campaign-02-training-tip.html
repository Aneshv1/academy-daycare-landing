If we could only teach one command for the rest of our careers, it wouldn't be sit. It wouldn't be stay. It wouldn't be come.

It would be Place.

Place means "go to your spot and settle there." That's it. Simple concept. But it solves an absurd number of problems.

Place fixes:

Jumping on guests — your dog can't jump on someone if they're holding Place across the room
Door manners — Place replaces the chaos of bolting and barking when someone knocks
Dinner time begging — Place at their bed while you eat. No more paws on your lap.
General impulse control — Place teaches your dog that doing nothing is an option. Most dogs have never learned that.

The key is duration. Most people teach Place for 10 seconds. A trained Place command means your dog holds it for 30 minutes, an hour, through distractions. That's where the real life change happens.

How to start at home: Pick a bed or mat. Lure your dog onto it with a treat. Mark and reward. Build duration slowly — a few seconds at a time. Release with a clear word like "free." Practice daily.
