One of the most common things we hear from dog owners: "He just wants to say hi to every dog!"

No. He doesn't. And making him do it is probably making things worse.

Here's the myth: dogs need to greet and play with every dog they encounter to be "well-socialized." Dog parks, sidewalk greetings, nose-to-nose meetings on a tight leash — all of it supposedly good for them.

Here's the reality: forced greetings are one of the most common causes of leash reactivity.

What actually happens during forced greetings:

Your dog learns that every dog they see = an interaction they need to prepare for
The leash restricts their body language, so they can't communicate properly — leading to miscommunication, tension, and snapping
One bad encounter creates a negative association that generalizes to all dogs
Over time, your dog starts lunging, barking, and losing it whenever they see another dog on a walk

What real socialization looks like: structured interactions with temperament-matched dogs in a controlled environment. Dogs learning to exist near other dogs calmly. Dogs playing with appropriate partners. Dogs resting around other dogs without arousal.

That's what happens at daycare — real daycare, not a room full of dogs left to sort themselves out. We match dogs by temperament and energy level. We manage play sessions. We enforce rest. Your dog learns social skills that actually transfer to the real world.

So the next time another owner on the sidewalk says "Oh they can say hi!" — you have full permission to say no. Your dog will thank you for it.

Better socialization starts here.
