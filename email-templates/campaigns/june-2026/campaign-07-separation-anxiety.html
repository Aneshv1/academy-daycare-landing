Separation anxiety isn't a training problem you can fix with a Kong and a YouTube video. It's genuine distress. Your dog doesn't understand where you went, when you're coming back, or if you're coming back at all. So they try to escape. They try to self-soothe. They destroy things not out of spite, but out of fear.

How daycare helps with separation anxiety:

Eliminates isolation. Your dog is never alone. They're with people and other dogs all day, every day.
Builds confidence. Dogs that are anxious alone often lack overall confidence. Structured socialization and success in a group builds it back.
Creates routine. Anxious dogs thrive on predictability. Same drop-off time, same structure, same people. They know what to expect.
Exhaustion helps. A tired dog handles alone time better. They come home and sleep instead of pacing and destroying.

We've worked with hundreds of dogs with separation anxiety over 15 years. The pattern is consistent: regular daycare attendance reduces destructive behaviour at home dramatically. Not because we "trained it out" — because the dog is no longer spending 8+ hours in isolation every day.
